زنده‌یاد باشم...

باغ درد... شاد شد...

پیرمرد... زنده یاد شد...

مهر مردِ مهربانِ شاد

مهرِ خامشی زِ لب گشود

بر نفس نهاد...
\
\
\
**تهران، خرداد ۱۳۷۵**

-----
-----

**English**
\
\
\
May I be one who is fondly remembered...

The garden of pain... grew glad...

The old man... became "the late"...

The kind, cheerful man's affection

lifted the seal of silence from his lips

and set it on his breath...
\
\
\
**Tehran, June 1996**
\
\
\
**Notes:**
- `Zendeh-yād` literally means "living memory," and in Persian it's the honorific for someone who has died, like "the late." He uses it both ways. First he wishes "may I be remembered," and then "the old man became `zendeh-yād`," meaning he died, but also that he now lives on in memory.
- `Mehr` means "affection," and the near-identical `mohr` means "seal." The last lines play on this: the man takes the seal of silence off his lips and puts it on his breath. He speaks or is remembered, but his breath, his life, falls silent. This double reading is one interpretation.
