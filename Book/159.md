وقتی که درویشی پریشان است

چون در هوای وصل جانان است

در هر بیابانی که راهی نیست،

هرسو که پایش رفت رَه آنست.
\
\
\
پا را چوبگذاری به راهِ دوست

رفتارِ تو کاری خوش و نیکوست،

تو رفته‌ای و جای پاهایت

شاید که راهِ چشمهٔ آهوست...
\
\
\
**پاییز ۱۳۸۵**

-----
-----

**English**
\
\
\
When a dervish is distraught,

it's because he longs for union with the Beloved.

In any desert where there is no road,

whichever way his feet go, that is the road.
\
\
\
When you set your foot on the Friend's path,

your going is a good and lovely deed;

you have gone, and your footprints

may be the path to the gazelle's spring...
\
\
\
**Autumn 2006**
\
\
\
**Notes:**
 jānān` (union with the Beloved), `rāh-e dust` (the Friend's path, meaning the way to God or the beloved).
- `Chubgozāri` reads oddly; I take it as `cho bogzāri`, "when you set [your foot]," with the two words run together in print.
