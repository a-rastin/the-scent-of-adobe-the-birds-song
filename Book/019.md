بر دارها...

خطّی و آونگی...

و نسیمی که بوی لاشه‌ها را...

تا... دهن‌درّهٔ کفتارها می‌بُرد...

و باد می‌آمد...

و... باد می‌آمد...

آنقدر تند که...

کفتارها مرد مصلوب را...

با موهای بلندش نشناختند...

آنگاه باد...

صلیبِ تنها را از روی تپه بُرد...
\
\
\
**۱۳۴۷**

-----
-----

**English**
\
\
\
On the gallows...

a line, and something swinging...

and a breeze that carried the smell of the corpses...

all the way... to the gaping jaws of the hyenas...

And the wind kept coming...

and... the wind kept coming...

so hard that...

the hyenas didn't recognize the crucified man...

with his long hair...

Then the wind...

carried the lonely cross away from the hill...
\
\
\
**1968**
\
\
\
**Notes:**
- `Dār` means gallows, and `āvang` is a pendulum. "A line, and something swinging" is a stark picture of a rope with a hanged body swaying from it.
- The long-haired man on a cross on a hill is a clear echo of Christ. Placed next to gallows and scavenging hyenas, it reads as a poem about executed or martyred people, and about how even the persecutors lose sight of them in the end.
- `Dahan-darreh` literally means "mouth-valley." It's a striking way of picturing the hyenas' wide-open jaws.
- In the Persian calendar the year is 1347, which is 1968.
