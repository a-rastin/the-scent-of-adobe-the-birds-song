در کوره راه‌ها...

آبی، خاکستری، سیاه

پیکرِ دهقانی‌ست با چوبدست بلندش.

سپید سیاه،

خرش را می‌گویم،

با خرواری از خارهای باران‌زده،

سیاهِ سیاه،

بَختش را می‌گویم

که همچون سایه

از کنارِ پاهایش روییده است.
\
\
\
**پاییز ۱۳۴۰ در راه بروجرد**

-----
-----

**English**
\
\
\
On the narrow dirt tracks...

blue, gray, black,

is the figure of a peasant with his tall staff.

White and black,

I mean his donkey,

with a heavy load of rain-soaked thorns.

Black, utterly black,

I mean his luck,

which, like a shadow,

has sprung up from beside his feet.
\
\
\
**Autumn 1961, on the road to Borujerd**
\
\
\
**Notes:**
- `Kureh-rāh` is a narrow, rough country path, the kind that runs between villages.
- `Kharvār` literally means "a donkey-load," an old unit of weight. Here it's carried by an actual donkey, so the word plays on itself.
- `Bakht-e siyāh`, "black luck," is the Persian idiom for misfortune or a wretched fate. The poem moves through the colors: the man is blue, gray, and black, the donkey white and black, and his fate entirely black.
