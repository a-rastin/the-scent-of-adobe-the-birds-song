در شبِ زلزله...

ماه در چشمِ کرمِ شب‌تاب

بارانی از مهتاب شد...

و... بر آوارها ریخت...

کرم شب‌تاب توان نداشت تا بشنود

غم‌انگیزترین هم‌سرایی را...

از مردمی که هرگز...

چنین یک دل با هم نخوانده‌اند...

تا... آخرین نفس...
\
\
\
**زلزله رودبار ۱۳۷۰**

-----
-----

**English**
\
\
\
On the night of the earthquake...

the moon, in the eye of the firefly,

became a rain of moonlight...

and... poured down on the rubble...

The firefly couldn't bear to hear

the saddest chorus of all...

from a people who had never...

sung together so wholeheartedly...

until... their last breath...
\
\
\
**Rudbar earthquake, 1370**
\
\
\
**Notes:**
- The Rudbar–Manjil earthquake hit Gilan and Zanjan provinces just after midnight on 21 June 1990 and killed tens of thousands. That date falls on 31 Khordad 1369, so the "1370" on the page doesn't match the quake. It may be when the poem was written, or the year may simply be off.
- `Āvār` means the rubble of collapsed buildings. The moonlight "pouring down on the rubble" picks up the moon-and-rain imagery that runs through the collection.
- `Tavān nadāsht tā beshenavad` is literally "had no strength to hear," meaning the firefly couldn't bear to listen.
- `Ham-sarāyi` means singing together, a chorus. The bitter irony is that people trapped under the rubble cry out "with one heart" (`yek del`), more united than they had ever been, until their last breath.
