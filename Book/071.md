به شوقِ روییدن

به دامِ آب افتادم...

و رود... رویای دوری بود...

و برکه... حسرتِ بی‌راهی...

نسیم دریا به خوابِ من می‌آمد...

و طعمِ لب‌هایم را شورتر می‌کرد...

به شوق روییدن...

به تاب افتادم... به دامِ آب افتادم...

و ابر... حسرتِ خورشیدِ تشنه...

و ابر... روح آبِ گریزان از آفتاب...

باران... خموش و غمناک...

همیشه در برگشت به ابتدای خاک...

در آرزوی عبور از هزار ساقهٔ تاک...

و داربستِ مو چه ساقه‌هایی داشت...

درخواب‌های کودکیم...

زیرِ سایهٔ نمناک، حبابِ ماه را بغل می‌کردم...
\
\
\
**۱۳۶۹**

-----
-----

**English**
\
\
\
Longing to grow,

I fell into the snare of water...

and the river... was a distant dream...

and the pond... the regret of having no way out...

the sea breeze came into my sleep...

and made the taste of my lips saltier...

Longing to grow...

I fell into restlessness... I fell into the snare of water...

and the cloud... the longing of the thirsty sun...

and the cloud... the soul of water fleeing the sunlight...

the rain... silent and sorrowful...

forever returning to the beginning of the soil...

yearning to pass through a thousand vine stems...

and what stems the grapevine trellis had...

in the dreams of my childhood...

under the damp shade, I would hug the bubble of the moon...
\
\
\
**1990–91**
\
\
\
**Notes:**
- `Be tāb oftādam` sounds a lot like `be dām oftādam`. `Tāb` means restlessness or agitation, and it can also mean twisting, the way a vine coils. `Dām` is a snare.
- `Bi-rāhi` means having no path. A pond is water with no outlet, so its "regret" is being stuck.
- `Dārbast-e mo` is a grapevine trellis, a familiar sight in old Iranian courtyards. Here the childhood memory is of cool, damp shade under the vines.
- `Hobāb-e māh` means "the bubble of the moon." It's probably the moon's reflection, round like a bubble, in water under the trellis, perhaps a pool or a puddle. The child hugs it.
