خواب می‌بینی

تو دوتاری هستی

من نوازندهٔ بی‌صبر و قراری هستم.

دشت ارژن خاموش

آتشی بر جانم

می‌زنم می‌خوانم

می‌پری از خواب

مانده بر پیکر تو

نقش انگشتانم.
\
\
\
**زمستان ۱۳۶۷**

-----
-----

**English**
\
\
\
You're dreaming

that you are a dotar

and I am a restless, impatient player.

The Arzhan plain is silent,

a fire burns in my soul.

I play, I sing.

You start awake,

and left on your body

are the prints of my fingers.
\
\
\
**Winter 1367 (winter 1988–89)**
\
\
\
**Notes:**
- A `dotār` is a long-necked Iranian lute with two strings, played with the fingers in Khorasan and Turkmen music. Since it's plucked by hand, the finger prints left on her body at the end make sense.
- `Dasht-e Arzhan` is a plain and wetland near Shiraz, known for its oak woods. It also comes up in Persian folk song. Here it sets a quiet, remote scene.
- `Bi-sabr o qarār` means "restless, without patience or calm," a common phrase for someone in love.
- `Mizanam` means "I play" (literally "I strike," as in striking strings), and `mikhānam` means "I sing."
