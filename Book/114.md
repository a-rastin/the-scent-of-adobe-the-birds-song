زیر پایم گُل...

آسمانم بارانِ نباریده... ابر...

و... دلم گلدانی...

ساقه‌هایِ عشق... برگ‌هایِ باد...

عطری از خاطرهٔ خنده تو...

در شبی هذیانی... خواب می‌بینم...

چوب سیگاری...

با نفس‌های تو پربرگ شده است...

باز هم این گلدان را...

در هوای تو رها خواهم کرد...

هرچه بادا... باد...

ساق پیرم... اما...

با نفس‌هایِ تو... گُل خواهم داد...
\
\
\
**محمودآباد ۱۳۷۲/۵/۲۱**

-----
-----

**English**
\
\
\
Flowers beneath my feet...

my sky, a rain not yet fallen... a cloud...

and... my heart, a flowerpot...

stems of love... leaves of wind...

a scent from the memory of your laughter...

On a feverish night... I dream...

a cigarette holder...

has burst into leaf with your breath...

Once again, this flowerpot...

I will set free in the air of you...

come what may...

my stem is old... but...

with your breath... I will bloom...
\
\
\
**Mahmudabad, 12 August 1993**
\
\
\
**Notes:**
- `Chub-sigār` is a wooden cigarette holder, a small, dry, dead piece of wood. In the dream, her breath brings it back to life and sets it sprouting leaves, much as she revives the poet's "old stem."
- `Harche bādā bād` means "come what may." The line plays on `bād`, which means both "may it be" and "wind," and that ties back to the "leaves of wind" a few lines up.
- `Dar havā-ye to` again means both "in your air" and "longing for you".
- `Hazyāni` means delirious or feverish, the kind of night when dreams run wild.
