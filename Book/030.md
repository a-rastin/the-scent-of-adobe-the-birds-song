دلم گرفته نگاه تو آفتابی نیست

به بیکرانِ کبودت زلال آبی نیست

ز سردی نِگهت قامت دلم افسرد

قیامتی است در آن دل که صبر و تابی نیست

به آب دیده بشویم غبار چشمانت

در این زمانه که در برگِ گل، گلابی نیست

نگاه می‌رَمَد از چشم من به گیسویت

اسیر تشنه به ظلمات و برق آبی نیست

دلیر باش و شبی جرئت گناهی کن

در این دو روزه اگر فرصت ثوابی نیست

بنوش ورنه چو نوبت به غم رسد بینی

درون ساغر شوقت نَمِ شرابی نیست

بیا به گرمی محجوب عشق دلبندیم

به دشت شب‌زده امید آفتابی نیست...
\
\
\
**زمستان ۱۳۶۶**

-----
-----

**English**
\
\
\
My heart is heavy, your gaze isn't sunny,

in your bruise-blue endlessness there's no clear water.

From the coldness of your look my heart's tall frame has withered.

There's a doomsday in a heart that has no patience and no strength left.

I'll wash the dust from your eyes with my tears,

in these times when there's no rosewater left in a rose petal.

My gaze runs from my eyes to your hair,

a thirsty captive in the darkness, and no gleam of water.

Be brave, and one night dare a sin,

if in these brief days there's no chance for a good deed.

Drink, or else, when sorrow's turn comes, you'll see

there isn't a drop of wine left in your cup of longing.

Come, let's bind our hearts in the shy warmth of love,

there's no hope of sunshine on this night-struck plain...
\
\
\
**Winter 1987–88**
\
\
\
**Notes:**
- This is a `ghazal`, the classic Persian lyric form, where every couplet ends on the same refrain, `nist` ("there is no"). I kept "there's no" at the end of most lines to echo that.
- `Qāmat` (stature) and `qiyāmat` (doomsday, resurrection) are a wordplay pair, a common device in classical ghazals.
- `Golāb` is rosewater. "No rosewater in a rose petal" means even beautiful things have lost their essence.
- `Zolmāt`, "the darkness," refers to the legendary land of darkness where the water of life was said to be hidden.
