من از تو سیر نخواهم شد...

کویر از باران...

صدای ساز عثمان می‌آید...

من از تو سیر نخواهم شد...

کویر تشنهٔ باران است...

ببین که می‌آید...

صدای خسته عطشان...

صدایِ عثمان است...
\
\
\
**۱۳۶۸/۱۱/۲۹**

-----
-----

**English**
\
\
\
I will never have enough of you...

as the desert never has enough of rain...

the sound of Osman's instrument is coming...

I will never have enough of you...

the desert is thirsty for rain...

see, it is coming...

the weary, parched voice...

it is Osman's voice...
\
\
\
**18 February 1990**
\
\
\
**Notes:**
- "Osman" is most likely Osman Mohammadparast (1931–2007), the dotar master from Torbat-e Jam in Khorasan. His playing and singing are known for a dry, desert-like ache.
- `Sir nakhāham shod` means "I will never be full or sated of you." The second line leaves out the verb, so it reads as "the desert, of rain."
- `ʿAtshān` is the Arabic-derived word for "parched." Having it rhyme with `ʿOsmān` ties the music to thirst. His voice is both the thirsty desert and the rain that's coming.
