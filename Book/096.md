تو را دوباره نگاه کردم...

دوباره مژگان را...

درون برکهٔ مهتاب شستشو دادم...

تو را نگاه کردم...

و پلک‌های تَرَم را...

در آبِ چشمهٔ دیدن...

چنان صفا دادم... که...

رنگ‌های صدایت را...

بگوش می‌دیدم...

و با نگاه ترم... بر لبانِ بیدارت...

هزار گفتهٔ گرمِ خموش می‌دیدم...

لبانِ تو می‌دید...

و چشم‌های تو در گوشه‌های بیداری...

ترانه‌ای می‌خواند...

کلام سبزِ ترِ عاشقانه‌ای می‌خواند...
\
\
\
**۱۳۷۰/۱/۱۳**

-----
-----

**English**
\
\
\
I looked at you again...

again I washed my lashes...

in the pond of moonlight...

I looked at you...

and my wet eyelids...

I cleansed so purely

in the water of the spring of seeing... that...

the colors of your voice...

I could see with my ear...

and with my wet gaze... on your waking lips...

I saw a thousand warm, silent words...

your lips were seeing...

and your eyes, in the corners of wakefulness...

were singing a song...

singing a fresh, green word of love...
\
\
\
**2 April 1991**
\
\
\
**Notes:**
- `Safā dādan` means to cleanse and also to make bright or joyful. Washing the eyes is a Sepehri-like image: `chashm-hā rā bāyad shost`, "the eyes must be washed," from *The Sound of Water's Footsteps*. This poem may echo it, but I can't say that for sure.
- `Sabz-e tar` means "fresh green." `Tar` (wet or fresh) is repeated through the poem: wet eyelids, a wet gaze, a fresh word.
