دِلِ شب بود و من خوابیده بودم

شدم بیدار، خوابت دیده بودم

کلافی بودم از ابریشمِ عشق...

که با مهرت به هم تابیده بودم...
\
\
\
**دههٔ ۱۳۷۰**

-----
-----

**English**
\
\
\
It was the dead of night and I was asleep;

I woke up, for I had dreamed of you.

I was a skein of the silk of love...

that I had twisted together with your affection...
\
\
\
**The 1990s**
\
\
\
**Notes:**
- `Kalāf` means a skein of yarn, and also, figuratively, a tangle of confusion (`kalāfe` means frazzled). So he's both a skein of love's silk and a little undone.
- `Tābideh` means "twisted" or "spun," the same thread imagery as the "twisted threads of water" in 154. `Tāb` also means endurance and radiance, the wordplay that runs through the book.
